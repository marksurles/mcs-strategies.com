Sculpt, rebuilt

In 1992, for my Ph.D. at UNC–Chapel Hill, I built Sculpt: a chemist grabs atoms on screen and the protein relaxes around the change in real time, under a full molecular-mechanics force field. Sculpt made the cover of Science and became Interactive Simulations, the company I founded and sold to Reed Elsevier.

In May 2026 I rebuilt it from the two original papers with AI coding agents, in about a week. Few people can check thirty-year-old scientific software line by line. Its author can, which made it the ideal benchmark.

Long-document Q&A

Clinical teams live inside 100+ page trial protocols, where the answer to one question spans a multi-page table, three sections, and an amendment. Generic chat tools lose that structure. This system keeps it: an AI agent navigates the document's own section tree and answers with clickable, page-anchored citations.

I built it twice: by hand in 2025 (custom retrieval, structure-preserving parsing, specialist agents per question type), then rebuilt API-first in 2026 with an evaluation set and a latency budget.
